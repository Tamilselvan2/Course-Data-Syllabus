Management — Concept & Functions
Very Important

Definition: Management is the process of planning, organizing, staffing, directing and controlling resources to achieve organizational goals effectively and efficiently.

Characteristics: Goal-oriented, continuous process, universal activity, group activity, dynamic function, and decision-making process.

  1. Planning
    Forward-looking process of developing mission, setting objectives, and determining how to accomplish them.
  2. Organizing
    Arranging, coordinating, and directing resources (men, material, money) to accomplish objectives.
  3. Staffing
    Recruiting, hiring, training, and keeping positions filled with qualified people.
  4. Directing
    Initiating planned action and guiding subordinates — "management in action."
  5. Controlling
    Channeling activities to ensure actual performance aligns with expected results.
📌 Exam Point

Management is a continuous, integrating, universal process focused on achieving organizational goals through people.

🏢
Types of Business Organization
Theory Heavy
Sole Proprietorship
Single owner, easy to start, full control, unlimited liability, small capital. Quick decisions but limited growth.
Partnership
Two or more owners, partnership agreement, shared profit/loss, mutual agency. More capital but potential conflict.
Joint Stock Company
Separate legal entity, limited liability, large capital, perpetual succession. Complex management but better growth.
Cooperative Society
Voluntary association for mutual benefit. Service motive, democratic management, equal voting rights.

Fayol's 14 Principles of Management
15-Mark Question
  1. Division of Work
    Work divided into smaller tasks for specialization and efficiency.
  2. Authority & Responsibility
    Managers must have authority along with responsibility.
  3. Discipline
    Employees must obey organizational rules and regulations.
  4. Unity of Command
    One employee should receive orders from only one superior.
  5. Unity of Direction
    One head and one plan for activities with the same objective.
  6. Subordination of Individual Interest
    Organization interest is more important than personal interest.
  7. Remuneration
    Workers should receive fair wages for their work.
  8. Centralization
    Balance between centralized and decentralized authority.
  9. Scalar Chain
    Chain of authority from top to bottom must be clear.
  10. Order
    Right person in the right place (material and social order).
  11. Equity
    Managers should treat employees with kindness and justice.
  12. Stability of Tenure
    Employees should have job security to perform efficiently.
  13. Initiative
    Employees should be encouraged to take initiative and suggest improvements.
  14. Esprit de Corps
    Promote team spirit, unity, and harmony among personnel.

Organization Structure & Chart

Organization Chart: Graphical representation showing authority and responsibility relationships from highest to lowest ranks.

  • Functional: Grouped by functions (HR, Finance, Marketing). Specialization but poor inter-department coordination.
  • Divisional: Based on product, region, or customer. Better focus but duplication of resources.
  • Matrix: Combination of functional and project. Flexible but dual reporting confusion.

Span of Control: Narrow (few subordinates) vs Wide (many subordinates).

Leadership Styles
  • Autocratic: Leader takes complete charge. Fast decisions but stifles creativity.
  • Democratic: Values input, employees participate. High engagement but slower decisions.
  • Laissez-faire: Employees have freedom. Encourages independence but can cause low control.
  • Servant Leadership: Focuses on employee growth and welfare. Better collaboration but time-consuming.
🧠 Leadership Traits

Intelligence, Self-confidence, Determination, Integrity, Sociability.

Organization Chart, Scalar Chain & Span of Control
2 Marks / MCQ

Organization Chart: Graphical representation showing authority and responsibility relationships from highest to lowest ranks.

  • Importance: Clarifies reporting structure, avoids confusion, improves coordination, defines authority clearly.
  • Scalar Chain: Chain of authority from top to bottom — the formal line of command.
  • Narrow Span: Manager has few subordinates. Tall organization, close supervision.
  • Wide Span: Manager has many subordinates. Flat organization, more delegation.
📌 MCQ Hotspot

"Narrow vs Wide span of control" • "Scalar chain is the chain of authority from top to bottom"

🔧
Taylor's Scientific Management

Uses scientific methods to improve worker efficiency and productivity.

  • Time Study: Analyzing time taken for tasks.
  • Motion Study: Eliminating unnecessary movements.
  • Standardization: Standard tools and procedures.
  • Functional Foremanship: Specialized supervisors.
  • Differential Piece Wage: Pay based on output.
📌 Principles

Science not rule of thumb • Harmony not discord • Cooperation not individualism • Maximum output • Development of workers

Economics — Concept

Study of how people and organizations use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants. Helps businesses decide what to produce, how much, and how to allocate efficiently.

🧮
Managerial Economics

Applies economic theories and analytical tools to business decision-making. Helps managers with pricing, production, investment, and profit maximization.

  • Demand analysis
  • Pricing decisions
  • Cost analysis
  • Profit planning
  • Risk analysis

Demand, Supply & Equilibrium
Numerical Zone

Law of Demand: Inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded. Price ↑ → Demand ↓

Law of Supply: Direct relationship between price and quantity supplied. Price ↑ → Supply ↑

Equilibrium: Where Qd = Qs

// Example: Find Equilibrium Qd = 100 - 5P Qs = 20 + 5P // Set Qd = Qs 100 - 5P = 20 + 5P 80 = 10P P = 8 // Substitute back Q = 100 - 5(8) = 60 // Answer: Equilibrium Price = 8, Quantity = 60
📌 Elasticity of Demand

Ed = (% Change in Quantity) / (% Change in Price)

Elastic → Large demand change | Inelastic → Small demand change | Perfectly Inelastic → Demand constant

MCQ: "If price and total revenue move in same direction → Inelastic demand"

Accounting Concepts & Conventions
Theory Heavy
9 Accounting Concepts
  1. Business Entity
    Business and owner treated separately.
  2. Money Measurement
    Only transactions in money recorded.
  3. Going Concern
    Business continues for long period.
  4. Accounting Period
    Fixed time period for accounts.
  5. Cost Concept
    Assets recorded at original cost.
  6. Dual Aspect
    Every transaction has debit and credit.
  7. Realization
    Revenue recognized when earned.
  8. Accrual
    Recorded when incurred, not when paid.
  9. Matching
    Revenue and expenses match in same period.
Accounting Conventions
  • Consistency: Same methods every year.
  • Conservatism: Avoid overestimating profits.
  • Full Disclosure: All important info disclosed.
  • Materiality: Only important info emphasized.
📌 Fill in Blank

"Accounting rules remain same according to Consistency concept."

💵
Profit & Loss Account

Shows net profit or loss during an accounting period.

// Formula Net Profit = Gross Profit - Expenses + Other Income // Example Gross Profit = ₹25,000 Office Expenses = ₹4,000 Insurance = ₹1,000 Net Profit = 25,000 - (4,000 + 1,000) = ₹20,000

Common expenses: Salary, Rent, Advertisement, Depreciation, Legal charges.

📋
Balance Sheet

Shows assets, liabilities, and owner's equity at a specific date.

// Fundamental Equation Assets = Liabilities + Capital // Example Total Assets = ₹1,60,000 Creditors = ₹40,000 Capital = 1,60,000 - 40,000 = ₹1,20,000

Current Assets: Cash, Stock, Debtors | Fixed Assets: Land, Building, Machinery

Current Liabilities: Creditors, Bills payable | Long-term: Bank loan, Debentures

⚡
Working Capital Management

Formula: Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities

Ensures smooth day-to-day operations.

  • Permanent W.C: Minimum capital permanently needed.
  • Variable W.C: Temporary capital for seasonal demand.
🏦
Sources of Finance
  • Internal: Retained earnings, reserves.
  • External: Bank loan, shares, debentures, venture capital.
🌐 Crowdfunding

Raising money from large public through online platforms. Types: Equity, Reward-based, Donation-based, Peer-to-peer lending.

🏦
Sources of Finance — Detailed
2 Marks / MCQ

Internal Sources: Retained earnings, reserves, sale of assets.

External Sources:

  • Shares: Ownership capital issued to public. Equity shares (voting rights) and Preference shares (fixed dividend).
  • Debentures: Long-term borrowed capital. Fixed interest rate, no voting rights.
  • Loans: Term loans from banks/financial institutions. Must be repaid with interest.
  • Venture Capital: Funding from investors for high-growth startups in exchange for equity stake.
📉
Elasticity of Demand — Types
2 Marks / MCQ

Measures how demand changes when price changes. Ed = (%ΔQ) / (%ΔP)

Elastic Demand
Small price change causes large demand change. |E| > 1
Inelastic Demand
Price change causes small demand change. |E| < 1
Perfectly Elastic
Tiny price change causes huge demand change. |E| = ∞
Perfectly Inelastic
Demand remains constant regardless of price. |E| = 0
📌 MCQ Hotspot

"If price and total revenue move in same direction → Inelastic demand" • "If they move in opposite directions → Elastic demand"

Project Management & Life Cycle
Very Important

Definition: Process of planning, organizing, scheduling and controlling resources to complete a project successfully within time and budget.

  1. Initiation
    Project idea and objectives identified.
  2. Planning
    Resources, schedule, budget and risks planned.
  3. Execution
    Actual project work carried out.
  4. Monitoring & Controlling
    Performance tracked and corrected.
  5. Closure
    Project completed and delivered.
📝
7 Steps in Project Planning
Repeated Question
  1. Define Objectives
    Clearly identify goals and expected outcomes.
  2. Identify Tasks
    Break project into smaller activities.
  3. Estimate Resources
    Determine manpower, materials, and budget.
  4. Develop Schedule
    Assign start and finish dates.
  5. Risk Analysis
    Identify risks and prepare solutions.
  6. Assign Responsibilities
    Allocate tasks to team members.
  7. Monitoring Plan
    Create methods to track progress.
🗂️
Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

Divides a project into smaller, manageable tasks. Improves task clarity, scheduling, and resource allocation.

📊
Gantt Chart

Graphical project schedule showing activities against time. Helps track progress, duration, and dependencies visually.

// Simple Example Task A = 2 Days Task B = 3 Days Task C = 2 Days Total = 2 + 3 + 2 = 7 Days Sequence = A → B → C
🚩
Milestone Chart

Shows important events or checkpoints in a project. Milestones measure progress and ensure accountability.

⏱️
Critical Path Method (CPM)

The longest sequence of dependent activities. Any delay here delays the entire project.

  • Zero slack activities
  • Determines project duration
  • Helps schedule optimization

Earned Value Management (EVM)
Numerical Zone

Measures project performance using scope, cost, and schedule. Identifies if project is ahead or behind.

// Key Terms PV = Planned Value (budgeted work scheduled) EV = Earned Value (actual completed work) AC = Actual Cost (money spent) // Formulas SV = EV - PV // Schedule Variance CV = EV - AC // Cost Variance // Example PV = 2000 EV = 1000 AC = 800 SV = 1000 - 2000 = -1000 // Behind schedule CV = 1000 - 800 = +200 // Under budget
💰
Cost Management

Types of Costs:

  • Direct Cost: Labor, raw materials (directly linked).
  • Indirect Cost: Electricity, administration (not directly linked).

4 Phases:

  1. Resource Planning
  2. Cost Estimating
  3. Cost Budgeting
  4. Cost Control
🔀
Managing Multiple Projects

Handling simultaneous projects with shared resources.

  • Resource Allocation: Identify bottlenecks and stagger schedules.
  • Prioritization: Rank by organizational value and urgency.
  • Standardized Tracking: Use uniform tools for high-level visibility.
📌 Case Study

E-commerce site + Digital marketing campaign sharing the same design team → stagger tasks to avoid overload.

🏉
Scrum Framework
15-Mark Question

Agile framework using iterative development through short cycles called sprints (1–4 weeks).

Product Owner
Defines product goals, manages backlog, maximizes product value.
Scrum Master
Ensures Scrum process is followed, mitigates distractions, facilitates daily stand-ups.
Development Team
Builds and delivers product increments. Adapts based on daily stand-up meetings (15 min discussions on progress, plans, blockers).
📌 Backlogs & Board

Product Backlog: List of all requirements maintained by Product Owner.

Sprint Backlog: Tasks selected for current sprint.

Kanban Board: Visual tracking with columns: To Do → In Progress → Testing → Done.

🔄
Agile Principles — 12 Core Ideas
2 Marks / Short

Know at least 5–6 of these for short answers and MCQs:

  1. Customer Satisfaction
    Highest priority through continuous delivery of valuable software.
  2. Welcome Changes
    Requirements can change anytime, even late in development.
  3. Frequent Delivery
    Deliver working software frequently, from weeks to months.
  4. Daily Collaboration
    Developers and business people must work together daily.
  5. Motivated Individuals
    Build projects around motivated people with support and trust.
  6. Face-to-Face Conversation
    Most efficient method of conveying information.
  7. Working Software
    Primary measure of progress.
  8. Sustainable Pace
    Constant speed indefinitely — no burnout.
  9. Technical Excellence
    Good design and agility enhance each other.
  10. Simplicity
    Maximize work not done — keep it simple.
  11. Self-Organizing Teams
    Best architectures emerge from self-organizing teams.
  12. Reflect & Adjust
    Team reflects on how to become more effective at regular intervals.
⚠️
Limitations of Agile
  • Difficult for large projects — continuous changes create complexity.
  • Requires skilled, collaborative, and experienced teams.
  • Scope creep — requirements may continuously expand.
  • Less documentation — detailed docs sometimes missing.
